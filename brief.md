## Overview 
A national Single Sign-On (SSO) service allows citizens to use one account to access multiple government websites and mobile applications. Software provides the authentication and authorization mechanisms needed to manage access securely. Artificial Intelligence (AI) can help identify suspicious login attempts, while mobile security protects users' devices and limits unnecessary permissions. Networking supports reliable communication between citizens and government systems and helps protect the privacy of DNS queries. Cybersecurity brings these controls together to reduce threats such as phishing, account theft, and unauthorized access. Data protection ensures that citizens' personal information is collected, stored, and processed responsibly under Oman's laws. These six fields work together to make the service secure, reliable, and trustworthy. Understanding the benefits and limitations of DNS over HTTPS (DoH) is an important part of protecting users' network privacy.                                                                                                         
## Software
OAuth 2.0 (Open Authorization 2.0) is an authorization framework that allows applications to access protected resources with permission. OpenID Connect (OIDC) is an identity layer built on OAuth 2.0 that enables applications to verify a user's identity using an ID token. For the government Single Sign-On (SSO) service, OpenID Connect should handle citizen login through the website and mobile app, while OAuth 2.0 should manage authorized access to government services and application programming interfaces (APIs).                                                                                                                                                                                                           Resource: OpenID Foundation – How OpenID Connect Works – https://openid.net/developers/how-connect-works/                                                 
## AI
The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) highlights the importance of identifying, measuring, and managing AI risks. Before launch, the authority should assess false alerts, missed attacks, bias, and privacy risks. The model should be tested with representative data to evaluate its accuracy and reliability. Clear responsibilities, human review, and continuous monitoring are also needed to identify errors and ensure the system remains effective after deployment.                                                                                                                                                                                           Resource: NIST – AI RMF Core – https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/                                                                
## Mobile 
Android uses an app sandbox and runtime permissions to limit access to sensitive data and phone features. The government login app should request only necessary permissions. On Android 6.0 and later, dangerous permissions must be requested while the app is running, when a feature needs access. Users can grant or deny these requests. The app should explain the purpose of each permission and avoid blocking basic login functions when optional access is refused.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                Resource: Android Developers – Request Runtime Permissions – https://developer.android.com/training/permissions/requesting                              
## Networking
Domain Name System over HTTPS (DoH) sends DNS queries and responses through an encrypted HTTPS connection. For government service users, this helps prevent network observers from reading or changing DNS requests during transmission. However, DoH does not hide users’ IP addresses from websites, prevent all online tracking, or encrypt traffic outside the DNS exchange. The DNS resolver may still see queries, so choosing a trusted provider remains important for privacy.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    Resource: Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) – RFC 8484 – https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8484                                           
## Cybersecurity
Phishing-resistant authentication links the login process to the genuine website, preventing attackers from reusing stolen credentials on fake pages. According to the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), Fast Identity Online (FIDO)/Web Authentication (WebAuthn), including passkeys, and Public Key Infrastructure (PKI)-based authentication qualify. In contrast, SMS codes, email codes, one-time passwords (OTPs), and standard push notifications are not phishing-resistant because attackers may trick users into sharing codes or approving fraudulent requests.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          Resource: CISA – Implementing Phishing-Resistant MFA – https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-01/fact-sheet-implementing-phishing-resistant-mfa-508c.pdf                                                                                                                                          
## Data
Under Oman's Personal Data Protection Law (Royal Decree 6/2022), authorities must handle personal data lawfully, inform individuals about data processing, and protect stored information against unauthorized access, disclosure, loss, or misuse. They must comply with applicable requirements for consent, data security, retention, and breach notification. However, Article 3 provides exemptions for certain government processing carried out under legal duties. The authority should verify which legal provisions and government-specific policies apply to its activities.                                                                                                                                                                          Resource: Ministry of Transport, Communications and Information Technology – Executive Regulations of the Personal Data Protection Law - https://mtcit.gov.om/ar/3/8/72/1035                                                                                                                   
## Misconception in My Track
A common misconception is that encrypting DNS requests means the DNS provider cannot read them. DoH protects queries while they travel between the user and the resolver, but the resolver must process the queries to provide DNS answers. This means encryption protects data in transit, not necessarily from the service receiving it. Choosing a trusted resolver and applying other privacy measures are therefore important. DoH should be viewed as one layer of protection rather than a complete privacy solution.                                                                                                                                                                                                                             Resource: Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) - RFC 8484, Section 8: Privacy Considerations -https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8484#section-8
